## ANIMALS  *(6 now — the thinnest category in the library)*

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.alleycat.org/resources/how-to-build-an-outdoor-shelter/)

## Build a winter shelter for the cats who live outside
when: This weekend
size: An hour
cost: Under $20
fact: A thirty-gallon plastic tote with a twenty-gallon tote nested inside, a styrofoam slab on the floor, and straw. Never hay, which holds moisture and goes mouldy, and never blankets.
why: the cats are already there, and the shelter is the difference between a hard winter and a fatal one

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/04/190401121814.htm)  
> Gunter & Wynne, PeerJ, 2019.

## Take a shelter dog home for one night
when: This weekend
size: A weekend
cost: Free
fact: Shelters run these as field trips or a doggy day out and hand you the leash, the food and the paperwork. An Arizona State study across five shelters found the dogs’ stress hormones fell during a one-night stay and stayed lower after they went back.
why: you are not adopting, you are giving one dog a night off

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.shelteranimalscount.org/stray-dog-intakes-spike-after-july-4th-new-data-from-shelter-animals-count-confirms/)  
> Shelter Animals Count’s own analysis of national 2021–23 data. They publish no percentage, so we should not invent one.

## Get the dogs in before the fireworks
when: A week or two
size: An hour
cost: Free
fact: July 5 is the single highest day of the year for stray dog intakes across the country. A text on July 2 to the three people on your street with dogs is the whole act: tags on, gate checked, dog inside with the radio on.

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://abcbirds.org/strategies/solutions-for-homes/)  
> The outside-the-glass ruling is the part nobody knows and the reason this works.

## Make one window safe for birds
when: Today
size: An hour
cost: Under $20
fact: Marks no more than two inches apart, on the OUTSIDE of the glass. Anything applied inside is invisible to a bird when the glass is reflecting. Non-toxic tempera paint counts.
why: more than a billion birds a year die on American glass

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.avma.org/javma-news/2017-08-01/pet-owners-shelters-and-clinics-check-chip)  
> Lord et al., JAVMA 2009, 7,700 stray animals.

## Get a found animal scanned before you post about it
when: Today
size: An hour
cost: Free
fact: Vets and shelters scan for free. Chipped dogs get home 52 percent of the time against 22 percent without one, and cats 38 percent against under 2. When a chipped animal still does not get home it is almost always the registration that is out of date, not the chip.

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://heartsspeak.org/how-photos-are-important-to-pet-adoption-a-study/)  
> HeARTs Speak study summary.

## Photograph the animals nobody is clicking on
when: A week or two
size: An hour
cost: Free
fact: Ask the shelter for its longest-stay animals and for its file-size rules before you go. Two thirds of adopters looked at photos online first, and two thirds of those called the photographs extremely or very important.
why: most listing photographs are taken through the bars of a kennel

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.idealist.org/)  
> One SPCA is currently recruiting two hundred people for laundry and dishes alone.

## Ask for the laundry and dish shift
when: This weekend
size: An hour
cost: Free
fact: Ask for the behind-the-scenes shift, not the dog walking one. Laundry, dishes and cleaning are the roles shelters struggle hardest to fill. Expect an orientation first, and ask whether there is an onboarding fee, because some shelters charge one.
why: everyone wants to walk the dogs and nobody wants to run the washer

> **CONFIRMED** &middot; [source](https://www.dogsondeployment.org/board-a-pet/)  
> Six-month average and the cost split both stated by the programme.

## Board a service member’s dog while they deploy
when: Needs a month
size: A village
cost: Free
fact: Dogs on Deployment matches boarders directly with military pet owners. The average stay is about six months and the owner stays financially responsible for food, vet care and supplies the whole time. You set your own availability.

## Leave the leaves where they fall
when: Today
size: An hour
cost: Free
fact: A couple of inches of leaf litter is what luna moths, swallowtails and woolly bears overwinter in, and thirty percent of native bees nest in hollow stems and dead wood. Rake the paths, leave the beds, and wait until late in the spring.
why: the one act of good that is done by not doing something

size: A weekend
cost: $20 or more

# Part three — never recommend

Reference, not candidates. The economics seat's list, with the evidence.

- **A canned food drive, as the default** — A can bought at retail and then sorted, dated and shelved by staff is the most expensive way to put food in a pantry. Cash buys at wholesale and buys what is missing. The exception matters: buying off a published top-needs list is a specific request, not a guess — which is why our existing idea survives.
- **Sending goods to a disaster nobody asked for** — The emergency management literature has a name for it: the second disaster. Trucks that should carry water carry junk, and somebody pays to landfill it. Cash and gift cards, always.
- **Orphanage volunteering, and voluntourism generally** — The Lancet called it misguided altruism. Paying to spend two weeks with institutionalised children creates demand that keeps children in institutions. UNICEF, ECPAT and Better Care Network are unanimous. Never, in any form.
- **Turning up at a soup kitchen on Thanksgiving without booking** — Some kitchens fill those shifts by August. Supervising a stranger for three hours costs real staff time. The act is asking which month they cannot staff.
- **One-off unscoped pro bono** — A day of unsolicited expertise that leaves a strategy document nobody asked for is a cost. The test: did they define the deliverable, or did you? Our existing “day of professional skill” should carry that question in its fact.
- **Bags of used clothing left at a charity’s door** — Most of it is not sellable and disposing of it is a cost centre. A specific closet asking for specific sizes in specific condition is the right shape — which is what our clothing-closet idea already is.
- **Donating a car through a for-profit intermediary** — The middleman’s cut can leave the charity with a fraction of the sale price. Sell it and give the cash.
- **Donating credit card points or airline miles** — Points redeemed to charity usually convert at a worse rate than cash back. Take the cash back and give the cash.
- **Judging a charity by its overhead ratio** — Charity Navigator, GuideStar and the BBB jointly published a letter telling donors to stop. Over a third of nonprofits report zero fundraising costs, so the ratio measures accounting, not effectiveness.
- **Micro-restricting a small gift** — “This $25 is for pencils only” obliges somebody to track $25 against a pencil budget. Below a few hundred dollars, restriction costs more than it directs.
- **Giving blood the day after a mass-casualty event** — The blood used that day was donated weeks earlier. Post-tragedy surges expire unused and the shelf is empty two months later. Our blood ideas should always point at scheduling, never at reacting.
- **Starting a new drive, fund or nonprofit for something that already exists** — The most expensive act of good available, and the one that feels best.
