Setup, step three of three

Where do you post?

The step already exists and already asks the question. The trouble is that it arrives with the answers filled in.

Instagram and Facebook are ticked before anybody has said a word. Somebody who only uses Facebook taps Start, and from then on the posting screen offers them an Instagram they never asked for.

As it ships

Two answers already given

The question is asked and answered in the same breath. Most people will tap Start without touching anything, and the app will have decided for them.

It is not a wrong guess — those two are the common ones. It is just a guess the app made about somebody it has not met.

What it costsA chip on the posting screen for a place they have never posted, every time, for a year.

A

Nothing assumed

Three empty boxes. The step becomes a real question, and the answer is theirs. Tick one, tick three, tick none.

The line underneath changes to say what the ticking actually does — and where to change it later, which nothing currently tells anybody.

What it costsOne more tap for the person who did want both. And Skip for now has to keep working, or somebody who skips ends up with no way to post at all.

Three things worth knowing before you rule

The hiding already works. Whatever is left unticked does not appear on the posting screen. Tested: turn two off and only the third chip remains.

Adding it back already works too. The same three rows live on the You screen, with the handle field beside each. Tick one and its chip returns. Nothing in the app currently says so, which is why the new line says it.

Somebody who ticks nothing still sees all three. That is deliberate and already built — an empty posting screen would be worse than a guess. So A is never a dead end; it only removes the assumption.

Rule it, or say what to change.
