A row that costs nothing

One tester read the old example and counted that every single thing on it cost money. Her words: “I have four dollars and two hands and you’ve asked for neither.”

You said to add a greyed-out zone that says help deliver the food. Here it is three ways — because the fourth grey line already says exactly that, then vanishes the moment somebody writes four things of their own. So the real question is whether you want one that stays.

A

In the list, always

A ghost row sits under whatever you’ve typed, however many things you add. Tap it and it becomes yours to edit. It never goes on the sheet unless you do.

The costThe list never looks finished. There is always one more line sitting under the last thing you wrote.

B

Its own quiet zone

Set apart under the list, with a line saying why it’s there, and room for more than one. Tap either and it joins your list.

The costIt says something out loud that A only suggests. Some people will read a gentle line as a nudge, which is the thing you didn’t want.

C

Leave it as it is

What shipped an hour ago. The free row is already the fourth grey example — it teaches the shape on an empty sheet, then gets out of the way.

The costAnybody who types four things of their own never sees it. The person most likely to build a wall of receipts is exactly the person who fills the list.

Rule by letter. A, B or C.
