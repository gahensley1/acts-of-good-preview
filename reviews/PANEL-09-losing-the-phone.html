50 Acts of Good · finding 16, and the new phone

Losing the phone

Three things drawn here: the You screen with the instructions moved into a popup, the popup itself, and Restore where somebody who has just lost their phone can actually reach it.

Ruled: 16B, the three steps, but in a popup rather than stacked on the screen. Restore goes on the page where you put your name. No explanation beside it — the words come after you tap it.

1 · The You screen, and the line that opens the popup

What is left on the screen: what a backup is, and a coral line that opens the rest. The eight-line grey block is gone.

L1drawn here

“How do I keep one safe?”

Her own question, in her own words. It reads like the app answering rather than instructing.

L2

Two other wordings for that line.

L2 — “Show me how, step by step.” Promises the shape of what opens, which is reassuring if you are nervous.

L3 — “Where should I put it?” Names the part people actually get stuck on, but it hides the tapping.

2 · The popup

Your three steps, and the Create a backup button lives inside it — so the instruction and the thing it instructs are in the same place.

3 · Restore, where you put your name

Today Restore from a backup lives on the You screen, and on a new phone you cannot reach the You screen until you have finished setting up and made a declaration. Somebody who has just lost their phone has to invent a year before they can get their real one back. This puts it on step one.

R1what you said

The button, and nothing else.

No paragraph. Anyone with a backup file knows what it is; anyone without one reads past it.

R2

One line above it: Had this app before?

Four words, not an explanation — it just tells the person the button is for them.

4 · And the words go here, after the tap

This box already exists — it appears once a file is chosen. It is the right place for the explanation, because by then the person has proved they have a file.

The added words, in the box: “Everything comes back exactly as it was — every act, every photograph, every person.”

Named, not changed: the left button says Keep it. On a phone with nothing on it yet, that is a strange thing to be offered. It wants a different word on this route.

Two letters and it gets built: L1, L2 or L3 for the line that opens the popup, and R1 or R2 for the setup.

Nothing here is built. The popup, the slimmed screen and the setup button are all drawings.
