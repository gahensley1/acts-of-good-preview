# The moment an act is completed

Brainstormed 7 September 2026. **Nothing here is decided.** The brief was G's:
*"what is the reward we can give them digitally that's awesome? A fountain of
hearts spewing out, like a firework?"* — with several versions so it is not the
same thing every time.

## The rule that solves the escalation problem

If act 3 gets fireworks, what is left for act 25? The answer is not rationing.

**Hearts rise and leave through the top. Confetti falls and leaves through the
bottom.** A per-act moment can then be as loud as you like and still never be
the finish, because **the finish is the only time the sky comes down.**

Second rule, doing most of the work: **the two big moments are the only ones
that stop you.** Halfway and the finish take the screen and ask for a tap.
Nothing on an ordinary act ever does — it happens around you, self-dismisses,
has no button, and you can swipe straight past and it will simply have happened.

Third: **default silent.** A phone in a quiet house at eleven at night is not a
place for a fanfare. If sound is ever on, one struck note. Never applause: it
puts an audience in the room, and an audience is the one presence this app must
never conjure.

## The idea argued hardest for

**A drawn hand at the foot of Your year holding one heart balloon per act.**
Logging an act sends one more up from below; the string tightens; the bunch
settles. About a second. No tap.

It is the only proposal that solves escalation **structurally rather than by
rationing**. The gesture is identical on act 24 and act 4 — nothing escalates —
yet act 24 is unmistakably bigger, because there is more of it in the picture.
It makes the halfway note visible without being told. And it hands the finish a
gesture that cannot be performed early: **the hand opens and the whole year goes
up at once.** At act 3 there is almost nothing to release.

It is made of the app's own drawing: the heart balloon and the hand already on
the card.

## Twelve moments, quietest first

**Still, no motion at all**
1. **The wall takes the photograph.** One of the act's photos becomes a pale
   ground behind the grid at 8-10% until the next act replaces it.
2. **The year card re-reads itself.** The line under the count changes and stays
   changed: *"Eleven acts. Marcus has been with you for four of them."*
3. **One true sentence**, assembled from the act's own facts, never repeated:
   *"The third one you have done with your daughter."*
4. **The rule is longer.** The gold hairline is drawn to the fraction of the year
   completed. No animation. Progress with no numbers.
5. **The frank.** A soft coral postmark over the first photograph carrying the
   date and an initial. Saved with the act. **Printed in the book** — the only
   one of the twelve you can still see in ten years, on paper.

**Moving**
6. **One balloon, released.** Rises from behind the new tile and out of the top.
   1.8s.
7. **The bunch gains one.** As above.
8. **The page turns.** A cream page sweeps right to left; the journal's spine on
   the tab bar is one hair thicker.
9. **The lamp.** No particles. The screen warms, light spreads outward from the
   new tile across the grid, and settles half a step warmer for the session.
10. **The names arrive.** Small coral cards, one per person, fan out, hold, then
    drop into the People tab. **A celebration in which your own name does not
    appear.**
11. **The sparkler.** A gold spark traces the act's number in one stroke and
    burns out. Spectacular and completely untakeable.
12. **The fountain of hearts.** G's idea: a jet from the drawn hand, ~120 hearts
    over 4s, every one leaving through the top.

## Variation that is true, not random

Random variety feels arbitrary. Vary on something real:

1. **How many people were with you sets the volume.** Alone: one balloon. Two or
   three: the names arrive. A crowd: the fountain. **This is the most important
   one — it moves the volume dial off progress and onto the thesis.**
2. **First act with a new person** → the names arrive, and that name is minted
   larger on the People screen, permanently.
3. **In honour of someone** → never confetti. One balloon, and their name in
   gold. Different in kind, not smaller.
4. **Done alone** → the still ones. *"Nobody was there and it still happened."*
   Solitude gets stillness, which is a different pleasure, not a lesser one.
5. **Photographs or none decides the material**, so an act with no pictures never
   looks like an act missing its pictures.
6. **A long gap gets the lamp** — the warm one. And it must **never mention the
   gap**, never say "good to see you". It just gets warmer and says nothing.
7. **A short gap gets the smallest thing.** You are mid-flow; do not stand in
   front of somebody already moving.
8. **The third act with the same person** → one still line.
9. **Writing at length earns being read** — a sentence of their own writing set
   back at them. The only reward writing has ever had.

Two rails: **an act logged after about 10pm gets a quieter tier**, and no mapping
ever makes a worse moment because of something the person failed to do.

## The anti-list

1. **A count-up or a percentage.** Turns a year with her friends into an
   arithmetic problem she is behind on.
2. **A badge, medal, trophy, "unlocked".** Implies a body standing outside the
   act judging whether it qualified. There is no such body.
3. **An auto-generated shareable card for every act.** The app has exactly one
   posting page at exactly the right moment. Broadcast every act and acts start
   being chosen for how they will look.
4. **A mystery box or a spin.** That is a variable-ratio schedule, the slot
   machine mechanism. It would work, and that is the problem with it.
5. **Applause or cheering.** Puts an audience in the room.
6. **"Your fastest act yet" / "three fortnights in a row".** A streak wearing a
   cardigan. It punishes the fortnight where a friend was ill.
