The ruled calendar

Option 3 with the lines thinned and the extra line across, and — further down — the six squares drawn hollow. True size on the left, blown up in the middle, on the month card at the right.

The box and the tabs never change — only the rules inside.

What each one is

3AAs you first saw it. Two down, one across, at full weight. Heavy inside a small box.
3BThe same lines at half the weight. Lighter, but four big cells still do not look like a month.
3CHalf weight and the second line across — six cells. My pick. It is the first one that reads as a month rather than a box with lines in it, and the thin rules keep the coral outline as the shape you see first.
3DThe same six cells a shade heavier, at three-quarter weight. Holds up better on an older screen; slightly busier.
3ENine cells — three down, two across. The most calendar-like of all, and at true size the cells are starting to close up.

Hollow squares, since you asked

Your message came through garbled, so this is my reading of it: the six squares drawn open rather than solid. If that is not what you meant, say it again and I will draw the right thing.

2AFilled — what is built now. Solid squares read instantly at 32px and rhyme with your year grid, where a completed act fills a square with coral. My pick.
2BHollow at 0.6. Lighter and more delicate. At true size the outlines start to close up into dots, so you lose the squareness that made them squares.
2CHollow at 0.8. Holds its shape better than 2B and still breathes. If you want hollow, this is the one that survives a phone screen.
2DHollow, one filled. The solid one is today. It carries a little meaning — but the icon does not otherwise claim to know what day it is, and at true size the difference is nearly invisible.

Four across, hollow, thinner — what you asked for

One more square on each row, so eight instead of six, drawn open with a fine outline. The box and the tabs never change.

AFour across, hollow, 0.5. My pick. Eight open squares. It is the first version that looks like a month laid out rather than a pattern, and at true size the outlines still hold their corners.
BThe same at 0.4. Finer and prettier blown up. At 32px it starts to ghost — take this only if you look at A on the phone and find it heavy.
CBigger squares, tighter gaps. More confident, slightly more crowded. Good if you want the days to carry more weight than the box.
DFour across, filled. For comparison only — eight solid squares is denser than six was, and the box starts to disappear behind them.
EThree across, hollow. What is built today, simply hollowed. Fewer, larger, calmer — but six cells never quite reads as a month.

The date in the box — your idea, and it beats both grids

Nothing inside but today’s number. Each row shows a single digit and a double digit, because the 3rd and the 25th are the two cases that decide it. Weights and sizes below.

ABold, size 9. My pick. Both the 3 and the 25 hold at 32px, which is the only test that counts.
CBold, size 8. More air around the number and slightly more elegant blown up. The 25 is tighter at true size, but it survives.
DBold, size 10. Fills the box. Confident, and the 25 begins to press against the walls.
B · ELighter weights. Handsome at 110px, thin at 32. On a coral stroke this fine, weight is what keeps the number readable.

What I think of the idea: it is the best of the three, and not close.

The grids and the squares were decoration — they made the icon look more like a calendar without telling anyone anything. The date is the only version where the icon says something no other pixel on that screen says. It changes once a day, silently, with no animation and no nagging, which is exactly the kind of life this app is allowed to have.

It also reads instantly, because every phone already has a calendar icon with a date in it. You are borrowing a convention rather than inventing one.

One thing to rule before it goes in. This icon appears in four places, and one of them is the button on an act that hands that act’s day to your phone’s calendar. Today’s date is the wrong number there — that button is about the act’s day, which may be next April. Three ways: put the act’s date in it, leave that one empty, or accept today everywhere and treat the icon as pure decoration on that button. I would put the act’s date in it — the icon then always says the day it is about.
