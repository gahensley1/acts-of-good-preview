The line under the code

ruled · “choose what you can”

“One thing” was doing quiet work, and your version lets it go — deliberately.

The old line said your one thing — singular, and deliberately so. It made the ask feel tiny, which is most of why people say yes to it at all. Your item(s) opens it to more than one.

That is a real trade, not a typo, and it cuts both ways: the generous person can now take three, which is exactly what Marcus and Sherri would do — and the hesitant person is being asked for a slightly bigger thing than they were yesterday. Nothing in the app stopped anyone taking two rows before; the poster just never said so.

Ruled in, knowingly. It also answers Renata, who counted that every row on that sheet cost money: what you can acknowledges what somebody has to give without ever mentioning it. No parentheses either — brackets on a poster read as a form.

What it does for you

18·5 · ruled in

The paper is now the mechanism, not just the advertisement.

Scanning the code a second time already recognises the person and offers them the way out — that was true before today and nothing anywhere said so. One line turns a thing that quietly worked into a thing people know they can use.

And it lands hardest exactly where the sheet came from: twenty-eight flyers on twenty-eight front doors. Those neighbours pass the paper every day. For them this line is the whole recovery story, and it needs nothing built.
