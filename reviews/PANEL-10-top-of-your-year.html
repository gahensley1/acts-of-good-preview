The top of Your year

Six versions of the same screen, at phone size, with three acts logged. Only the top of the card changes — everything below it is untouched.

Your argument, and it holds: the letter comes first, then setup. By the time anyone reaches this screen they chose the number themselves. The case against isn’t day one — it is day two hundred, at 9 of 25 in week 30, when the same screen stops reflecting a choice and starts reporting a shortfall. The letter is read once. This is read every morning.

The four numbers, and why they are not the same thing

“3 of 25” is your number. You chose it, it is on the card, it is how Jessica opens every post. Your own settled rule protects it.

“Week 1 of 52” and the bar are the app’s judgement about your number. Nobody asked for a pace clock. That same rule says the app’s judgements come out — it is what took away “one to catch up”.

Which is why several of the versions below keep the count and drop the pace. That may be the whole fix.

The most severe. The count moves below the grid where you can go and find it. Honest to the letter, and possibly too austere.

G · Your idea, drawn — the month leads, and it lists what is planned

The month card comes to the top. Under it, only what is planned — a date and an act, in your calendar’s order. Nothing completed is listed; the grid below already shows all of that. The count survives as one quiet line at the foot of the card.

Gthis is the one

September, and what is coming.

It stops being a score and becomes an agenda. It answers what is next rather than how far behind am I, and every line on it is a real thing with a date, not a measurement.

G·emptythe honest test

And the month with nothing in it.

This is the version that decides whether G works. A screen whose whole job is “what is coming” has to survive a month where nothing is. It reads as an invitation rather than a gap — but it is the one to look hardest at.

How I feel about it: it is better than any of my six, and it is better for a reason worth naming.

Every one of mine still looked backward — the last act, the count, the reason. Yours is the only one that faces forward, and forward is the only direction with nothing to be behind on. It is also the only one that gives the planning half of the app a reason to exist on the first screen.

Two things it costs you. The pace clock goes, which I think is a gain. And the count drops to one grey line — which is a real change to the thing you chose in setup, so it is yours to accept or push back on.

One open question: does an act planned for next month show here, or does the card only ever hold September? Showing the next one along means the screen is never empty. Holding to the month is cleaner. I would hold to the month and let the empty state do its job.

Say a letter. G is yours and I think it wins. A–F are above it for comparison, not competition.

Nothing here is built. The screen on your phone is unchanged.
