50 Acts of Good · panel finding 16

The backup words

The screen tells Eileen she will lose the year, then gives her instructions she cannot follow. She does not know what Files is. That is the screen she put the phone down on.

Your direction: keep naming a location. So none of these drop iCloud Drive — they add the words around it that make it followable.

What is there now

Two things are wrong with it, and only one is the words.

First, it is a single grey block of eight lines. Somebody anxious about losing a year reads the first line and stops.

Second, it names two things she has never heard of — Save to Files and iCloud Drive — without saying what either one is or what she will be looking at when she gets there.

Nothing else on this screen needs touching. The sentence above it — because it never leaves the phone, it can go with the phone — you already ruled on, and it stays.

Three ways to fix it

16A

Same paragraph, more hand-holding.

Smallest change. Keeps the shape, adds what she will see and what Files actually is.

How to keep one safe. Tap Create a backup. A panel slides up from the bottom with a row of icons — that is your phone asking where to send the file. Choose Save to Files, which is your phone’s own filing cabinet, then pick iCloud Drive. On an Android phone, choose Google Drive. Anywhere that is not only this phone. Once a month is plenty, and again after any act you would hate to lose.

16Bmy pick

The same thing, as three steps.

The density is half the problem. Steps let her look away at the phone, then find her place again — which is exactly what happens at step two.

How to keep one safe.

  1. Tap Create a backup.
  2. A panel slides up from the bottom with a row of icons. Choose Save to Files — your phone’s own filing cabinet.
  3. Pick iCloud Drive and tap Save. On an Android phone it is Google Drive.

Anywhere that is not only this phone. Once a month is plenty, and again after any act you would hate to lose.

16C

Steps, with the easier road named first.

Same row of icons carries Mail. For somebody who has never opened Files, sending it to herself is one tap and she already understands where it went. iCloud Drive is still named, just second.

How to keep one safe.

  1. Tap Create a backup.
  2. A panel slides up with a row of icons. The easiest is Mail — send it to yourself, and it sits safe in your inbox.
  3. If you would rather file it away, choose Save to Files and pick iCloud Drive. On an Android phone it is Google Drive.

Anywhere that is not only this phone. Once a month is plenty, and again after any act you would hate to lose.

16A, 16B or 16C. All three keep the location named. None of them touches anything else on the screen.
