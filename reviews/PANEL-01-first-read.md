# The testers on build 3N

Seven people, 8 September 2026. They saw the live app, twelve screens each, and they did not confer. Each was given their own year to run: Dana 12 acts, Trent 10, Renata 20, Brianne 25, Marcus 50, Paula 52, Sherri 100.

---

## The short version

**Nobody is a yes to this app.** Three said yes and all three meant *yes to one feature*. Two said no outright. Two said a thin yes and named the half they would never touch.

- **Trent — NO.** Out at the posting screen, and honest that he knew at the first screen.
- **Brianne — NO.** "By week six I'd have 3 logged and 40 done."
- **Dana — thin yes.** Will use the grid and the journal, will never open Post It once.
- **Renata — thin yes.** Three or four of her twenty will make it in.
- **Marcus — yes, to the sign-up sheet only.** Would abandon the year-of-25 part by act nine.
- **Sherri — yes, to the sign-up sheet and to nothing leaving the phone.**
- **Paula — yes,** and she told us not to count it. "I am the ideal user. Treat my yes as the weakest finding here."

---

## The five things more than one of them hit

**1. The backup bar covers the buttons at the bottom of every screen. All seven.**
Every single one. It sits over the tab labels on all twelve screens and cannot be dismissed. Marcus: *"somebody shipped without scrolling their own app."* Paula: *"that is not a design decision, that is something left switched on."* This is the cheapest fix on the list and it is the first thing every one of them saw.

**2. The placeholder story is going out under their names. Five of seven.**
*"It went about as well as these things go"* is sitting in multiple journal entries at once, and it is pre-filled into the caption ready to post. Marcus: *"I did not write that. It was queued to go out under my name."* Paula: *"in a printed book that is humiliating."* Sherri: *"it's the app being wry so it doesn't have to be sincere."*

**3. The letter is hers, and they know it. Four of seven, unprompted.**
Sherri: *"I'm being invited into someone's commemorative project rather than given a tool."* Paula: *"it is her book with my acts pasted in."* Dana: *"I'm allowed to use it. I'm not who it's for."* Renata: *"that's a hobby, and the app is shaped like her hobby."* None of them were told anything about Jessica. They worked it out from the screen and it changed how they read everything after it.

**4. "No one is keeping score" is on the same screen as the score. Two of seven, stated flatly.**
Brianne: *"'No one is keeping score' is on the same screen as a progress bar, a count, and a running dollar total. The whole app is score."* Sherri: *"Don't hand me a counter and then apologize for it."*

**5. Three names for one screen. Five of seven got lost.**
Plan & Log, In the works, Acts in the works, Log an act. Marcus and Trent both said they could not tell which screen they were meant to be in.

---

## The sign-up sheet is the best thing you have built and it is buried

Five of the seven said it unprompted, including all three of the ones with real organising to do.

Marcus, who would actually run one: he publishes from the church car park, twenty people claim inside an hour, **and he cannot see who claimed what.** No live list, no notification, no way to close it when there is enough, no way to add an item after publishing, no quantity so nine people don't all bring rolls. *"I will find out in the worst possible way, with a table full of rolls."*

Sherri, the same, in her words: *"Right now I can't tell from the app who's bringing what, and that's the entire job."*

Both also flagged: **the sheet asks for a phone number on a page you pin to a public noticeboard.** Marcus: *"That's my cell on a noticeboard."*

---

## Three sentences to read twice

> **Dana:** "For someone whose list is two, this app is a slow accumulation of empty chairs, and the more warmly it's written the more it stings, because it's clearly not trying to."

> **Trent:** "You've made a very nice thing for people who are comfortable being seen being good. I'm not, and I don't think that makes me worse at it."

> **Brianne:** "This is built by someone with time. Someone who turned fifty and had evenings. Every screen assumes I can finish a thought."

---

## Where they want opposite things — these are yours to rule

**A. Posting.** Dana and Trent want the whole posting layer gone, chosen once at setup and never mentioned again. Marcus and Sherri want it *stronger* — Sherri wants a sheet that posts straight into a Facebook group. Both cannot be the default.

**B. Money.** Renata wants the cost tally removable entirely, not zeroed: *"if I enter $0 twenty times, the app's biggest number reads zero all year, which will look, on my own screen, like I did nothing."* Brianne, independently: *"putting a dollar figure on it makes me feel small."* Paula never mentioned money once. It is invisible to the comfortable and load-bearing to everyone else.

**C. The pace line.** Renata: *"I don't need a second timesheet."* Sherri wants it to say 100 and finds "plenty of room" patronising. Brianne would delete the app rather than look at 3 of 25.

## Each of them, in their own words

### Dana Whitlock, 46 — two friends, no children. 12 acts.

**Stopped at:** the People screen. *"Everyone who's been part of an act, and anyone who's offered."* Nobody has offered. She read it twice and put the phone face-down.

**What she wants:** "who it was for" made a real field instead of buried and greyed — half of what she does is for one person. Ideas filtered by *can I do this alone*. Repeat acts, so taking the same neighbour's bins out every Tuesday is one thing continuing, not seven identical entries padding her book.

**Her five:** 1. Kill the backup bar after one dismissal. 2. "Who it was for" equal to "who's in it with you." 3. Let her hide posting entirely at setup. 4. One name for the drawer. 5. Filter ideas by "alone."

**Doesn't like:** the hashtag. And: *"the letter says the real thing was time with the people she loves, and then the whole app is built on that assumption. Invite someone. Ask a friend along. Who's in it with you. Make a sign-up sheet."*

---

### Marcus Bell, 38 — choir director, enormous reach. 50 acts.

**Stopped at:** the backup bar. He has 41 names in his phone for the Thanksgiving repast and trusts none of it to one device.

**What he wants:** **groups.** His worlds don't mix — choir, the Thursday pantry crew, his listings, his husband's people — and one flat "People" list is not how he holds them. Reminders that go out *from* the sheet two days before. A "what's still unclaimed" line he can screenshot into a group chat, because that's what actually moves the last three items. An anonymous mode for the family receiving, because nobody wants their name on why the casseroles are needed.

**His five:** 1. A live claims view, and one tap to close the sheet. 2. Backup that happens without him. 3. Sign-up as its own thing in the nav. 4. Quantities and a duplicate guard. 5. Never write a caption he didn't write.

**On the flinch test: it passes.** *"No wife assumed, no scripture, no 'traditional' anything, the hand on the card is changeable. Nobody made me brace."*

**Doesn't like:** `BUILD 3N` printed on the main screen. And the hashtag: *"I'd be embarrassed to have my choir see that under my name."*

---

### Brianne Cotter, 30 — four children, one on the way. 25 acts.

**Stopped at:** "Who's in it with you?" — her people are 7, 5, 3 and 18 months and they are in nearly all of it. *"Am I adding my own children as collaborators every time?"*

**Her count of the work:** Log an act is two typing fields and about eight taps, two minutes if nobody needs her, *"which is never."* In the works is nine sections and four or five minutes: *"that is not a phone screen, that's a desk."*

**What she wants:** children as a whole category, not collaborators — and let her print *their* year, not hers. Twenty ideas a preschooler can actually do. Voice entry. A photo from the camera roll that logs itself. Logging four at once on a Sunday night. Someone else able to log for her.

**Her five:** 1. A ten-second log — name it, save it, done. 2. Backup that doesn't need her to understand iCloud. 3. Children first-class. 4. Voice. 5. Pull the sign-up sheet out on its own.

---

### Renata Salas, 41 — two jobs, three children, four spare dollars. 20 acts.

**Stopped at:** "Spent so far $64 · Your share $64" in week one. *"That's my gas for a week and a half, sitting at the top of the screen like a scoreboard."*

**She counted the ideas.** 32 of them; **17 need money she does not have** — adoption fees, bird feeders, gift cards, doughnuts, a recurring monthly gift. *"A recurring gift as a suggestion is almost funny."* The free half reads like busywork beside it.

**What she wants:** acts she can do while already doing something else, because her only free time is stacked. Acts a six-year-old can physically do. A cost filter with free as the default. And: *"something for people who receive kindness too. I've had groceries left on my porch. There's no version of me in this app."*

**Her five:** 1. Let cost be **off**, not zero. 2. Filter ideas by cost. 3. Kill the pace line. 4. Two-tap logging. 5. Backup nag out of the tab bar.

**Doesn't like:** *"I felt like the charity case in a kindness app. Half the ideas were things somebody might do for me. That stung, and I'd deny it if you asked."*

---

### Paula Reinhardt, 52 — architect, empty nest, wants the printed book. 52 acts.

**Stopped at:** the top of Your year. "7 of 25" with "week 1 of 52" beneath it and "week 1 of 52" again top right. Read it three times. Then two identical dollar figures, one black, one red, unexplained.

**Genuinely well made, her words:** the journal page — *"the eyebrow, title, script date, 'with Priya' — that hierarchy is right, and the script date is the only decorative move in the app that earns itself."* The act list is properly tabular. The backup writing is the best writing in the app.

**A hair off:** the first screen's button straddles the gold frame while carrying its own outline, three frames arguing. The date ribbons nearly touch the tile numerals. Two buttons on Log an act wrap to two lines and break in different places. The optional detail ticks read as disabled because they're grey on white.

**The card is the failure.** *"'good' appears twice in one lockup, in two faces. The heart's line weight and the hand's do not match. The rule is centred, the lockup above it is not, so the whole thing hangs left. A third of the card is empty. And it is white on white — on a feed it will have no edge at all."*

**Her five:** 1. Toast off the tab bar. 2. **Redraw the card** — one weight, one "good", a border so it exists on a white feed. 3. Fix the header: one count, one week, say what "your share" means. 4. One name for one screen. 5. "Write it up" and "Change anything" are the same job under two labels.

---

### Trent Okafor, 34 — saw his ex do it. 10 acts.

**Stopped at:** Post it. *"That's the door shut. I am not posting a hashtag about fixing someone's fence."* But he was honest that it started earlier — screen one, third paragraph, "shared it on social media." *"I knew then. I kept going because I wanted to be wrong."*

**Would he tell a friend?** *"I wouldn't. That's the real answer. If someone found it on my phone I'd say my sister sent it."*

**What he wants:** a quiet mode chosen on day one — no posting, no card, no hashtag, never mentioned again. One-tap logging. Logging after the fact. Something for **skills rather than money**: *"I fix things. 'Fixed a fence' is the only one on that list that looks like me."*

**Does it read as made for women?** *"Yes. The gold script, the balloon, the letter from Jessica in Savannah, 'what good can you spread.' Not one thing in it looks like it expects me. My ex could post that card and it'd look right on her feed. On mine it'd look like a cry for help."*

**He liked:** the tile grid, instantly. The cost split. Handing the day to the phone's calendar instead of nagging. The sign-up sheet — *"that's a real tool."*

---

### Sherri Vance, 44 — nine thousand women in her group. 100 acts.

**The language note, which is why she is on the panel:**

- *"create a record of the good you put into the world"* — *"Nobody in my house has ever said that. That's a phrase from a certain kind of magazine."* She'd write: **a record of who you served this year.**
- *"What good can you spread?"* — *"Spread is for butter."* She'd write: **who can you serve this year?**
- *"a little good"* is printed on her card and on a hundred journal pages. *"Read from the outside it's small and a bit precious."* Let people choose or leave it blank.
- *"the rest ripens whenever it likes"* — say "you can fill in the rest later."
- **The ideas filter has no church.** Thirty-odd ideas, and *"not one of them is a church, a shut-in, a widow, a family in the hospital, a funeral meal, a nursery volunteer. That is where the majority of organized service in my town actually happens, and it isn't a category."*

**Where she'd change nothing:** "In honor of." "Who's in it with you?" "They're remembered for next time." "For a neighbor who could use a hand." "Dear neighbors," "Anonymous act. Just the card posts." And the backup page — *"the plainest, most respectful writing in the app. Write the rest of it like that."*

**Would she share it with nine thousand women?** *"Probably not, and not with a fuss. I'd try the sign-up sheet, it would work, and then I'd never post the app itself — because when I recommend something, I'm vouching for its voice too, and 'the good you put into the world' would read to a good number of them as being spoken to slightly from above. That's the whole reason. Not politics. Tone."*

**Her five:** 1. Sign-up sheet: quantities, claims, "2 of 6 taken", a closing date, editable after publishing. 2. A number over 50 without hunting, set before the grid is drawn. 3. Real placeholder text, or none. 4. A church filter and a dozen ideas behind it. 5. Let a sheet post to a Facebook group.

---

## What I would fix first, if it were mine

1. **The backup bar off the tab bar.** Seven for seven. An afternoon.
2. **Delete the placeholder story.** An empty page is honest; a shrug printed three times in one book is not.
3. **The sign-up sheet gets a claims view** and comes out of the bottom of an act. It is the one thing on this panel that made three organisers lean in.
4. **A quiet mode at setup** that removes posting for good. It converts Trent from a no and costs the others nothing.
5. **Let money be switched off, not zeroed.**

---

## Honest note on the test

Two complaints in these notes are my fault, not the app's. Trent and Renata both said their settings didn't match what they'd chosen — that is because I handed them all the same pre-filled account of 25 acts under the name Sam Ortega. The app did not lose their setup. **Discount those two lines.**

Everything else they saw is real and on the live build.
