What three seats found, and what needs your letter

The code seat read the parts of the app nobody had ever reviewed. Two design seats looked at the build as pictures. The code seat found something live in 5H that loses a year — that is already fixed and is not on this page as a question. Everything below is what is left, ranked, with a recommendation on each.

Already built and tested — build 5I, no ruling needed

  • Starting a new year is no longer undone. You finished a year, started a new one, closed the app — and the old year came back. Every time, for ever. It is the worst thing this app could do and it was live.
  • Deleting your last act is no longer undone — under a warning that said it could not be undone.
  • The rescue no longer deletes every photograph. When the app struggled to read a journal it saved the words, deleted all the pictures, and then told the person to back up.
  • A repair no longer takes photographs with it.
  • Restoring a backup clears the old spare copies, so the thing you just replaced cannot come back over it.
  • The saved file now records how many acts it should hold, so the app can tell "something ate this" from "the person emptied it" instead of guessing. Guessing was the cause of all of the above.
  • The test battery now lives in the repo. It never did — it lived in a cloud workspace and vanished with every session, so "181 checks pass" was a sentence nobody could re-run. 35 checks, in your folders, runnable for ever.

C — The code seat

Based on SQLite and Obsidian. It read the persistence, the card drawing and the plan code — and named what it did not read, which is most of the sign-up sheet and almost all the styling.

C1Confirmed

Reminders do not exist, and they are inside the code you told me to cut

The "remind me that morning / the day before" choices are only reachable through a screen nothing opens any more. Inviting someone else with a calendar invite still works — different path, alive. But a person cannot remind themselves of anything. I need this before I can cut.

C2Confirmed

The backup file does not say which version made it — and this is only cheap today

Every backup has claimed "version 1" since the beginning, through several changes to what a backup holds. Today that is fine, because there is one version. The day the app is in two stores with people on different versions, someone makes a backup on the new one, restores it onto an older phone, and everything the new version added is deleted without a word. Backups are the only lifeboat. This can be fixed now for almost nothing and cannot be fixed later, because the old builds will already be on people's phones.

C3Confirmed

"Save for later" puts an idea in a square you cannot open

On Ideas, Save for later puts the idea into a numbered square on the grid. Tapping that square does not open it — it starts a blank act. Close the app and reopen it and the whole thing sorts itself out and the idea appears properly on the shelf. So a saved idea lives in a different place depending on whether you have restarted. It is also the one thing that can still put an unfinished ghost into the grid you ruled was the finished year.

C4Confirmed

The app never says when it has put an older copy back

It already knows — it makes a note to itself and then never reads it. Silence is right for a repair that worked perfectly. It is wrong for one that has moved someone's journal backwards by minutes or a session, because then the person's own memory is the only evidence anything happened.

C5Confirmed

The three spare copies collapse into one for a person who dips in and out

A new spare copy is taken every time the app opens. Somebody who opens it, logs one act and closes it takes a fresh one every visit — so after three visits all three copies are of the same few minutes. "Three deep" implies you can reach back. The person who would benefit most gets the least.

C6Confirmed

Two decorative script fonts are built into the app and nothing uses them

About 55KB of dead weight, and two more other-people's licences riding along for no benefit. Given your ruling that there are no new script faces, they are also a loaded gun — the rule is currently enforced only by nobody having typed their names.

C7Confirmed

No licence notices ship with the app

The barcode-drawing code and all four typefaces are other people's work under licences that require their notice to travel with the software. There is a one-line credit for the barcode code and nothing else. This is store paperwork, not engineering, and it is the kind of thing that is trivial now and awkward at submission.

S — The screens

Seat 9, based on Work & Co, MetaLab, Ramotion and Clay. Its verdict: the build is good, and the journal is the best-made screen in it. Its one structural note is that three screens each repeat one loud element until it stops meaning anything.

S1Confirmed
Both design seats found this independently

The picture you post is smaller than the one you drew

On the card screen the artwork fills the square. The file that lands on Instagram has a white border all the way round and the artwork sits smaller inside it — about four-fifths strength. It was done deliberately, to stop the card being cropped in a story, and for stories it works. But a square posted to the feed is never cropped, so every feed post is paying for a crop that is not happening, next to photographs that are not paying it.

S2Confirmed

What you approve is not what you post

Separate from S1 and true whichever way you rule it. The preview on the card screen and the file that gets made are drawn by two different parts of the app, and they disagree about how much air is around the artwork. The preview flatters it.

S3Confirmed

The fifty grid reads like warning tape

Eleven finished squares, each with a white diagonal stripe and a date, all touching. One stripe is lovely. Eleven line up into a single repeating diagonal and the top of your year reads as hazard stripes rather than eleven good things. You ruled on 8 September that every stripe stays and I am not asking you to undo that — every date should stay. The only question is how loudly each is said.

S4Confirmed

There are two reds and they sit next to each other

On the card screen "act 11 of 50" is the artwork's warm coral and the button below it is a deeper brick red. On Your year the thin progress line is the warm coral and the finished squares under it are the deeper red. The reason is sound — white words on the warm coral are hard to read, so a darker one was made for anything carrying words. But the finished squares do not carry words on the red and were changed anyway.

S5Confirmed

The ideas page is a wall of red buttons

Around forty cards and every one has the same filled red "Start it now". A page where everything shouts is a page where nothing does. This is not about removing the second button — that was your 13 September fix and it was right. It is only about weight.

S6Confirmed

In the journal, the loudest thing on every page is the least important action

Every page carries the same dashed box with an envelope in it, and dashed boxes read as "something is missing here". Scrolling the journal, that box is what you see eleven times — while "Write it up", the thing you actually want people doing, is a small underlined line above it.

S7Confirmed

Dates are written five different ways

A finished square says 3-14-26, the line under it says 5 Sep, the journal says March 21, 2026, a message says Monday 21 March 2026, a date button says 20 Sep '26. Two put the month first, three put the day first. Nobody will name it, but it is part of why the app feels slightly less made than the card does.

S8Confirmed

The grid ends with one square alone on the last row

Because the declaring act sits first, the fifty that follow are pushed along by one and number 50 ends up on its own with four empty spaces beside it. It reads as a mistake rather than as an ending.

S9Confirmed

The backup offer lands on top of the grid

The black bar sits across a row of finished squares for nine seconds, once. Not permanent damage, but it covers the thing the person came to look at, at the moment they are looking. And nothing stops the little black "pick a day" label being on screen at the same time, so two black things can appear together.

S10Confirmed

People, with nobody in it, is the emptiest screen in the app

Two grey paragraphs and one outline button on plain white, then a large area of nothing. Every other screen has a ground, a warmth, a card. This one has none, and a new person lands on it early.

K — The card

Seat 12, the still image. Its verdict: the lockup is good and the white ground is a real advantage in a feed of photographs — but as a series it does not survive fifty postings, because the only thing that changes is a small count, and a count is not a fact about a day.

K1Confirmed

The card hangs on two different centres

Draw a line down through the middle of "50", "acts of good" and "fifty-three" — they all sit on one axis. "act 11 of 50" does not. It sits about a tenth of the card's width to the right of it, on the centre of the whole square, and the thin rule does the same. The top half hangs off the words; the bottom half hangs off the square. Small on one card, recurring across fifty.

K2Confirmed on what changes

Fifty of these are fifty of the same picture

The same audience sees this square fifty times and the only difference is one small coral number. A stranger scrolling cannot tell act 3 from act 41. A count is an index — it says where we are in a list, not what happened. The card already knows one other thing that is true, specific, and different every single time, and it needs no new element and no new colour: the date.

K3Confirmed

The person's own photograph is hidden behind the card

When an act has photographs, the card always goes out first and the photographs are the slides after it. In a feed the first slide is the only one most people ever see — so the one picture that is genuinely different every time never gets seen, and the identical card is what does.

K4Confirmed

The card sits high, and the declaration card has a third of its face empty

There is noticeably more white below the act line than above the balloon — the composition is pushed up against the top of the square and the bottom looks like a shelf nobody put anything on. It is worst on the declaration card, which carries no rule and no act line at all, and which is the card most likely to be shared widest.

K5Confirmed

The book and the card are strangers

The book is already a good object — cream paper, a fine ruled plate, a coral act line, a short gold rule, the date in the script face. But it shares almost nothing with the card: the card is white, the book is cream; the card's rule is long and black, the book's short and gold. And the balloon, hand and heart — the one thing anybody would recognise — appear nowhere in the book at all, not even on the title page.

K6Confirmed

The book is set to two different page sizes

The file tells the printer the book is US Letter, 8.5 by 11 inches. A note in the same file describes it as 8.5 by 8.5 square, and the journal pages you read on the phone are laid out square. So what a person reads on screen and what comes out of a printer are currently different shapes. This wants ruling before a printer is ever chosen.

K7Recommendation

The twelfth seat's third name should change

You named Max Shkret for the still-image seat. He is the weakest name in the group by a clear margin — no acquisition, no auction record, no named campaign, and he has never made a series or kept a daily practice. The seat proposes On Kawara: one ground, one typeface, one rule, made across forty-eight years, where the only thing that ever changed was the date. He answers our hardest question directly and answers it against us — his changing element was never an index, always a fact about the specific day. That is the whole argument behind K2.

What both seats said not to touch

  • The card lockup. Not one element of it. Everything proposed above is about the foot, the axis and the margin — nothing inside the mark moves.
  • The white ground. It is the only reason this square stops a thumb in a column of photographs. Every card in this category eventually gets a colour, a gradient or a shadow because white looks unfinished on a designer's screen. Refuse it every time it comes back.
  • The journal's warm paper and its gold hairline. The best-made screen in the build.
  • The script on the journal dates. Not two places — three. A journal is the one place a date should look written by hand.
  • Act 0 as an ordinary tile. The moment it gets special treatment it becomes a badge, and this app does not do badges.
  • Four things under the surface that are already right: the app clears the notch and the home bar from one setting; Reduce Motion is handled properly — the movement stops but the message still arrives; the poster and book have real print rules and the book is laid out on screen at printed proportions; and every square in the grid is a real button with a spoken label.
  • Do not add more motion, and do not put a handle, a logo, an app name or a QR code on the card. It is the person's year, not our advertisement.

What nobody has looked at yet

Silence is not approval, so: the setup screens and Jessica's letter have never been reviewed and are the first thing a new person sees. Most of the sign-up sheet's app side is unread, and it is the only part that touches a network. The printed poster has never been judged. Nobody has looked at the app at the narrow end of the phone range, where the idea filters are already running off the edge.

And the two things that can only be settled on a real phone: whether a card posted to a real feed at full strength actually beats one at four-fifths, and whether the book prints at all.
