13Eileen · Ruth

Twenty-six squares under a heading that says twenty-five.

Act 0 sits in the grid as an ordinary square, and nothing on that screen says what it is. Eileen counted the squares twice with her finger. Ruth called it a straight contradiction between the heading, the grid and the counter.

You have already ruled that the zero square is normal — no gold outline, no separate line above the grid. So this is only about whether anything on the screen explains the extra square.

13ALeave it. The heading counts the year; the declaration isn't one of the twenty-five, and nobody has to be told that.
13BThe heading says it: "25 acts, and the day you said so."
13CThe counter under the grid says it, and the heading stays clean: "1 of 25 · and your declaration."
16Eileen

The backup tells her she'll lose everything, then gives her instructions she can't follow.

The You screen says to choose Save to Files and put it in iCloud Drive, over a coral line warning that the year will be lost otherwise. She does not know what Files is. That is the screen she puts the phone down on.

She is the person who does the most good and would be lost first. This is the only place in the app where a warning is followed by a wall.

16ALeave the words as they are.
16BStop naming the place. "Your phone will ask where to keep it. Anywhere you can find it again is fine."
16COffer the two routes she'd actually use, by name — email it to yourself, or save it to this phone — and drop the cloud words entirely.
17Eileen · Kayla, separately

Sending it to one person is the footnote. For two of them it's the only road.

On the posting page, "Send it as a note to someone" comes last and smallest, after everything else. Both women said, without conferring, that it is the only route they would ever use.

Priya called the private send offered at equal weight a real piece of moral design — but it isn't at equal weight yet.

17ALeave the order as it is.
17BEqual weight — post it and send it to one person, the same size, side by side.
17CAsk once, in setup, which one you want first — and put that one on top from then on.
18Priya

The person you helped is in the same list as the friend who came along.

The People screen holds everyone who's been part of an act, and tagging draws from that one list. So the app ends up pointing at getting a handle onto the name of somebody who was helped.

Priya's words: the app has worked hard to protect you from guilt and not at all to protect the people around you from becoming inventory. She says this one fix makes several of her others unnecessary.

18ALeave it as one list.
18BTwo lists on the same screen — who you did it with and who it was for. The "for" side is never asked for a handle and never offered for tagging.
18COne list, but each person carries which they were, and tagging only ever reaches the "with" side.
19Priya

The book's back page is an index of everyone in your life who needed something.

The printed book ends with "the names it was all for". Her line: Ray took ten seconds to say yes to his bins going out. He did not say yes to being on page 114 of a book.

Nothing is built yet, so this costs nothing to decide now and a great deal to decide after the first book is printed.

19AKeep the index as planned.
19BThe index names only the people who helped — never the people helped.
19CNo names page at all. The book carries the acts and the stories, and a name appears only where you wrote it yourself.

You have ruled this once already. "Act n of N" is on your settled list. Two of the other three settled items — half acts, and "How did it go?" — you have since overturned yourself, so it is fair to put this in front of you one more time. Saying 20A closes it for good.
20AIt stays. Closed, and not raised again.
20BThe number comes off the card. It stays in the caption and in the journal, where it is yours rather than on display.
20CThe number stays, and one switch in You takes it off every card for people who don't want it.
