# Journaling, memory-keeping and the year-end

Researched 7 September 2026 against Day One, 1 Second Everyday, PYM, Dabble Me,
Journey, Diarium, Reflectly, Stoic, Apple Journal, Streaks, Way of Life, Done,
Duolingo, Finch, Letterboxd, Goodreads.

## Making writing feel optional rather than owed

The risk: somebody does 25 kind things and owns 25 unwritten stories.

**The strongest answer, and we have the data for it already.** Apple Journal
builds the entry from the grouped photographs, the place and the time **first**,
and only then offers a writing prompt. **The entry is never empty, so there is
nothing outstanding.** We capture title, date, who and photographs at the moment
of logging. *(Built 7 September: an unwritten act now renders as a finished page.)*

**Never render an absence as an absence.** Way of Life logs each day Yes / No /
**Skip** — the third state exists so a blank can mean "didn't apply" rather than
"you failed."

**Attach writing to a moment that already exists.** Dabble Me has no app at all:
you reply to an email. *"Most people don't stop journaling because they don't
care. They stop because life gets busy. Instead of asking you to make time, it
fits into the day you already have."*

**Write next to your own past, not into nothing.** Every Dabble Me email carries
an old entry with its photo above the reply box.

**Make the prompt visibly refusable.** Day One's daily prompt can be shuffled,
dismissed for today, or turned off forever. The escape hatches are what make it
a suggestion rather than a demand.

**The counter-example, and the most important finding here.** Finch removed all
punishment and still produced guilt, because it kept asking. A reviewer quit at
six weeks: *"the more it wanted me to come back, the less I wanted to."* And
*"I was excited to check things off, and felt guilty when I didn't."*
**Frequency of asking is itself a punishment, independent of tone.** No warmth in
the copy survives being sent twice a week. **Our no-notifications decision is
doing more work than any gentleness in the wording could.**

## Photographs

- **Group them into a moment, not a pile** — Apple Journal assembles an outing.
- **Offer to find the missing ones ON REQUEST.** PYM's SmartFill and 1SE's
  "Smart-Fill a month in one tap". Both run when asked, never on a schedule.
  An offer is generous; an unrequested notice that you have gaps is an accusation.
- **Argue for the ordinary photograph.** PYM: private capture changes what people
  shoot. *"Challenge apps optimize for the best photo of the day. A private photo
  journal optimizes for honesty."* The blurry one where everyone is laughing is
  the correct photograph.
- **Print quality is what makes a sparse entry feel serious.** A one-line entry
  printed beautifully reads as deliberate.

## The year-end, if we ever build one

*(Note: our closing page was ruled on 7 September — catchphrase and one line, no
names, no count. These mechanics are recorded for a future year-in-review, not
for that page.)*

- **The bar sits below where the person already is.** Letterboxd: ten films.
  Goodreads: three books. Chosen so everyone receiving it has already qualified.
  **A report card is scored against a target; a gift has a minimum you cleared in
  February.**
- **It arrives; you do not fetch it.** Both email it.
- **The record can be repaired right to the end, and they say so.** Goodreads:
  stats update "until Dec. 31, so every last book gets counted." Letterboxd
  publishes a get-ready procedure weeks ahead. **The pre-announcement is itself
  the gentlest possible reminder.**
- **It is a sequence, not a page.** Pacing and reveal beat density.
- **It is composed of nouns, not numbers.** Goodreads shows the covers.
- **What we cannot copy:** Wrapped's feeling depends on simultaneity — everyone
  gets theirs the same day. We have no cohort and should not chase one. What we
  have instead is that **the year-end can be an object.**

## Printing, and the shelf

**Day One**: $19.99 for 50 colour pages then $0.10/page, hardcover +$5,
5.5x8.5in, 384-page limit, **iOS only**, **free users can print**, and **you can
export the PDF from inside the preview**. Privacy framing is explicit: automated,
no human involvement, files deleted after shipping.

**1SE does not print books** (the store sells notebooks and t-shirts). **PYM**
does — a FUJIFILM yearbook up to 600 moments, price not published.

PYM's argument, and it is a good one: **"The shelf is the point."** The physical
object accumulating year on year is what keeps people at it, not a counter.

**For us:** showing somebody how many pages their book currently runs to, and
letting them see it as a book long before the year ends, gives every ounce of the
"keep going" pull with none of the dread — **because a book with gaps is just a
book.**

## Streaks: the evidence, for saying no

- **Renfree et al., CHI 2016** (16 users of Lift): streaks "create a dependency:
  on-going app use is often required to achieve lasting change... when the
  extrinsic motivation is removed, the impetus for the behavior disappears."
  A participant: *"I don't want to face up to the fact I'm failing, but then I can
  get in a mode where I stop doing any of them."*
- **Silverman & Barasch, J. Consumer Research 49(6), 2023**: an intact streak
  changes behaviour **independently of the actual history** — the same past,
  displayed two ways, produces different choices. People treat maintaining the
  log as a goal in itself. Harm is amplified when the break is self-attributed.
- **Lally et al., EJSP 40(6), 2010** (96 people, 12 weeks): **"missing one
  opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit
  formation process."** Time to automaticity ranged 18 to 254 days.
- **Duolingo** now sells Streak Freeze, Streak Repair and auto-repair behind the
  paid tier: an economy for managing anxiety the streak manufactured.

**Two statistics that circulate widely appear to be INVENTED** — a "CHI 2020
study on streak anxiety" and a "63% more likely to quit" figure. No paper found.
**Do not use them.**

## The gentle alternative, with better evidence than the streak has

**Milkman et al., Nature 2021.** A megastudy: 54 programmes, 61,293 gym members.
**The top performer was a micro-reward for returning after a MISSED workout** —
about 16 cents, +0.34 weekly visits, a 23% lift. The surveyed experts did not
predict it.

**The moment of maximum leverage is the moment after the gap, and the correct
response to it is welcome rather than penalty.** A streak does the exact opposite.

**For us this means one rule:** an act logged three weeks late slots into the
journal in its proper place, dated correctly, indistinguishable from one logged
on the day. **No "logged late" marker. Ever.**
